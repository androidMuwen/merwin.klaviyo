Welcome series

Mock project — Fernweh Goods

Goal: convert new subscribers into first-time customers before their initial interest cools.

Signupâ†’ Email 1: Welcome + brand storyâ†’ Wait 1 dayâ†’ Email 2: Bestsellers + social proofâ†’ Wait 2 daysâ†’ Email 3: Discount expires in 48hâ†’ Exit on purchase

Why this structure

The first email earns trust before it sells anything — no discount, just what the brand is and why it exists. The second email does the selling, using bestsellers rather than a catalog dump so the choice feels curated. The third email introduces urgency only once goodwill is established, so it reads as a nudge rather than a hard sell.

Segmentation

  • Suppressed for anyone who purchases mid-flow
  • Discount code swapped for a "you're already a customer" variant if they'd bought before signing up
  • Klaviyo profile property flags flow completion for later win-back logic

Would report on: flow revenue per recipient, placed-order rate by email step, and where in the sequence most conversions land.

Abandoned cart flow

Mock project — Fernweh Goods

Goal: recover carts without training shoppers to always wait for a discount.

Added to cartâ†’ Wait 1 hrâ†’ Email 1: item reminder, no discountâ†’ Wait 23 hrsâ†’ Email 2: FAQ / objection handlingâ†’ Wait 24 hrsâ†’ Email 3: 10% off, expires tonightâ†’ Exit on purchase

Why this structure

Leading with a discount teaches every shopper to abandon on purpose. The reminder comes first, the objection-handling email addresses shipping cost or sizing doubts second, and only the third email offers a discount — and only to whoever is still undecided after two honest attempts.

Segmentation

  • High-cart-value shoppers get a free-shipping offer instead of a percentage discount, to protect margin
  • Repeat abandoners are capped at one discount send per 30 days

Would report on: recovered revenue, recovery rate by step, and discount redemption rate to check margin impact.

Black Friday campaign calendar

Mock project — Salt & Reed

Goal: spread demand across three weeks instead of concentrating it — and losing it to inbox fatigue — on one day.

Pre-BFCM (2 weeks out)

  • Teaser: something's coming, no details
  • Early access: VIP segment gets a 24-hour head start
  • List-growth push via a giveaway popup

BFCM week

  • Launch email at 6am local time, segmented by timezone
  • Mid-sale reminder to openers-who-didn't-buy only
  • Last-chance send, cutoff time stated explicitly

Post-BFCM

  • Thank-you email — no ask, just confirms the order and sets shipping expectations
  • Cross-sell based on what they bought
  • Win-back queued for 45 days out for anyone who browsed but didn't buy

Why this structure

VIP early access rewards the list you already earned instead of treating every subscriber identically. Segmenting the mid-sale reminder to openers-who-didn't-buy avoids re-fatiguing people who already said no by not opening.

Would report on: revenue by send, unsubscribe rate against a normal week, and VIP segment revenue share versus general list.

Segmentation & personalization

Mock project — general framework

Goal: the same campaign, worded differently depending on who's opening it.

Segments built

  • Engaged vs. unengaged (opened/clicked in 90 days)
  • First-time vs. repeat customers
  • VIPs (top 10% by lifetime spend)
  • Category interest, from browse and purchase behavior
  • Lapsed — no purchase in 120+ days

How the message shifts

  • VIPs get early access framing, never a generic discount
  • First-timers get reassurance copy (returns, shipping, reviews)
  • Lapsed customers get "what's new since you left," not a blanket coupon

The point isn't more segments — it's fewer, sharper ones that actually change what gets said.

Subject line optimization

Live data — client campaign

Goal: improve urgency without leaning on generic discount language.

Before

After

"Your 20% off expires tonight"

The first version reads as one of dozens of identical discount subject lines. The second attaches the same offer to a real deadline, which is what actually drives opens — loss aversion, not the number itself.

How I work

The same process behind every case study above.

  1. Audit

    Understand the store, the audience, the existing email program, and what "working" would actually look like.

  2. Strategy

    Define which campaigns, flows, and segments matter most, and in what order to build them.

  3. Copy & design

    Write and design toward one conversion goal per email — never several competing ones.

  4. Build

    Implement responsive emails and automation logic directly in Klaviyo.

  5. QA

    Test links, discount codes, personalization, mobile rendering, and flow branching before anything sends.

  6. Analyze

    Read performance past the vanity metrics and turn it into the next thing to test.
